## “I got a bill” / payment workflow

All four exact 2026 EOCs contain protections against improper provider billing for covered services. The safe operational pattern is:

`Bill received -> identify provider/service/date -> locate claim -> read disposition -> determine documented member responsibility -> use plan billing/payment workflow -> resolve/escalate`

Key principle: **a bill by itself does not establish that the member owes it.** For covered network services, the EOCs generally direct providers to bill the plan and give members a process for sending the bill to Aetna or requesting reimbursement/payment review. If payment or reimbursement is denied, appeal rights apply.

## Appeals and grievances — do not use a universal linear ladder

A trainer note summarized appeals as `Aetna G&A -> Maximus -> State Hearing`. The exact 2026 EOCs show that this is too broad to publish as one universal sequence.

### Common Medicare-side structure

Across the four EOCs:

- Level 1 appeal starts with the plan.
- Level 2 Medicare review may go to an **Independent Review Organization / Independent Review Entity (IRO/IRE)**.
- Standard grievances are generally resolved as quickly as the case requires, no later than **30 calendar days**; under the EOC-defined circumstances, an expedited grievance is resolved within **24 hours**.
- Higher Medicare appeal levels can continue beyond Level 2 depending on the issue and applicable thresholds.

### Medicaid/state variation

| Plan | State-side finding |
|---|---|
| Michigan `H9314-001` | EOC defines a **State Hearing** for disputed Medicaid services and separately describes IRO/Fair Hearing paths |
| Illinois `H9771-001` | EOC describes both IRO and **Fair Hearing** review; Fair Hearing requests are tied to the Level 1 decision notice and state process |
| Virginia `H1610-001` | EOC identifies a **State Fair Hearing** through Virginia DMAS, including an expedited/fast Fair Hearing path when health requires it |
| New Jersey `H6399-001` | EOC includes an **IURO** route and identifies **Maximus Federal** in that context, plus a separate Fair Hearing process |

### Consequence

**Maximus is not a universal Level-2 label for every plan and every appeal type.** The issue must first be classified as Medicare vs. Medicaid, medical vs. Part D, standard vs. expedited, and then routed using the exact plan/state workflow.
