## First classifier

`ISSUE -> Medicare vs Medicaid -> medical/service vs Part D -> coverage decision vs appeal vs grievance -> standard vs expedited -> exact plan/state route`

### What the terms mean operationally

- **Coverage decision:** the initial plan decision about coverage, payment, or certain restrictions.
- **Appeal:** a formal request to review/change an unfavorable coverage decision.
- **Grievance / complaint:** dissatisfaction that is not itself a coverage/payment dispute; examples include service, access, timeliness, behavior, or quality concerns.
- **Fast / expedited:** a separate urgency track that applies only when the EOC criteria are met.

### Fast medical appeal

The exact EOCs use an expedited/fast appeal when delay could seriously harm health or the ability to function. A physician's statement that the member's health requires the fast track generally triggers expedited handling under the plan rules.

Do not use a fast-appeal workflow for a pure request to reimburse a service already received; payment/reimbursement has its own decision and appeal path.

## Medicare Level 2 — IRO/IRE, not automatically “Maximus”

For Medicare-covered services/items, an unfavorable Level 1 decision may move to an **Independent Review Organization (IRO)** / **Independent Review Entity (IRE)**. The exact EOCs often automatically forward qualifying Medicare cases to Level 2.

Therefore:

**Do not label Level 2 simply as `Maximus` unless the exact plan/notice names Maximus for that specific review.**

## Medicaid / state Level 2 varies materially

### Michigan

For Michigan Medicaid-covered services/items, the EOC provides a state **Fair Hearing / State Hearing** path. For issues potentially covered by both Medicare and Medicaid, the EOC describes an IRO Level 2 review and the ability to ask for a state Fair Hearing.

### Illinois

Illinois uses State Fair Hearing routes that depend on the Medicaid service/waiver category.

Examples in the exact EOC include:

- HFS Bureau of Administrative Hearings for standard Medicaid items/services, the Aging Waiver/Community Care Program, and Supportive Living Facility waiver matters: **1-855-418-4421**, fax **1-312-793-2005**, `HFS.FairHearings@Illinois.gov`.
- DHS Bureau of Hearings for certain Home Services Program waivers: **1-800-435-0774**, fax **1-312-793-3387**, `DHS.HSPAppeals@Illinois.gov`.

The adverse-decision letter should determine the correct state route; do not choose a state hearing office by memory alone.

### Virginia

Virginia's EOC provides a **State Fair Hearing** through the Virginia Department of Medical Assistance Services (DMAS), including a fast/expedited Fair Hearing path when the health criteria are met. Use the decision notice and current controlled routing for the live request.

## Grievances / complaints

### Common timing

The exact EOCs generally require the plan to address a grievance as quickly as the member's case requires and **no later than 30 calendar days**. Under the EOC-defined expedited-grievance circumstances, the plan responds within **24 hours**.

Part D grievance filing may have its own 60-day timeliness rule; check the exact issue and EOC/current controlled guidance.

## Do not confuse these workflows

| Caller says | Correct first bucket |
|---|---|
| “They won't cover my surgery/service” | Medical coverage decision / appeal |
| “My drug was denied” | Part D coverage determination / redetermination unless Medicaid-only drug rules apply |
| “I paid and Aetna won't reimburse me” | Payment coverage decision -> appeal |
| “The rep/provider was rude” | Grievance, not appeal |
| “They're taking too long” | Determine whether complaint/grievance about timeliness or missed decision deadline; expedited grievance may apply in defined cases |
| “They are reducing a service I already get” | Medical/Medicaid appeal + check continuation-of-benefits deadline immediately |
| “I want an outside review” | Identify program/service and current appeal level before choosing IRO, IURO, QIO or State Fair Hearing |

## High-risk deadline reminder

For reductions/stops of ongoing services, the EOCs contain **continuation-of-benefits / aid-pending deadlines that can be shorter than the general appeal deadline**. Example: Michigan, Illinois and New Jersey EOCs describe continued coverage when the Level 1 appeal is requested within the applicable notice/effective-date window, including a 10-calendar-day rule in the cited sections.

Therefore the desk aid should say:

**SERVICE BEING REDUCED/STOPPED NOW? -> check the adverse notice immediately for continuation-of-benefits deadline before giving the general appeal deadline.**
