# 2026 Aetna D-SNP Provider / Network / PCP Workflow

**Status:** Exact-EOC public-source workflow baseline  
**Updated:** 2026-08-08  
**Scope:** Michigan `H9314-001`, Illinois `H9771-001`, Virginia `H1610-001`, New Jersey `H6399-001`

> **Network status is plan-specific and can change.** Check the exact plan directory/current internal provider tool and, when an appointment is imminent, confirm participation with the provider. Aetna's current Medicare directory page says directory data is updated frequently but may be delayed by system work/outages.

## High-speed provider branch

`PROVIDER ISSUE -> exact plan/state -> PCP or specialist? -> current network status -> accepting new patients? -> referral? -> PA? -> continuity/out-of-network exception? -> resolve / PCP change / authorization / care coordination -> notes`

## PCP requirement and change workflow

All four exact 2026 EOCs require/select a **network PCP** to oversee the member's care. If the member has not selected a PCP, the plan may assign one.

Across all four plans, the EOCs say the member may **change PCP for any reason, at any time** by contacting Member Services.

The common PCP-change workflow is:

1. Check that the requested PCP is in the exact plan's current network and accepting new patients.
2. Before changing, identify specialists or services currently coordinated by the old PCP, such as home health or DME.
3. Member Services updates the membership record and tells the member the effective date.
4. The plan issues an updated ID card reflecting the new PCP information where applicable.

Plan Member Services:

- Michigan: **1-855-676-5772**
- Illinois: **1-866-600-2139**
- Virginia: **1-855-463-0933**
- New Jersey: **1-844-362-0934**

## Specialist referrals — important HMO clarification

All four exact EOCs state that the PCP may refer the member to a specialist, **but the member can go to any specialist in the plan network without a PCP referral**.

This is operationally important because the label **HMO D-SNP does not mean "every specialist visit needs a PCP referral."**

However:

- a particular service/test can still require **prior authorization**;
- some benefit-specific rules may still require an order/referral for the service itself; and
- out-of-network specialist care follows a different authorization rule.

Desk-aid rule:

**IN-NETWORK SPECIALIST?** `No general PCP referral requirement -> still check service-specific PA/order rule.`

## Out-of-network medical care

The exact EOCs generally require use of network providers. Unauthorized routine out-of-network care may be denied, leaving the member responsible for the cost.

Common exceptions include:

- emergency care;
- urgently needed care;
- Medicare-certified dialysis while temporarily outside the service area; and
- medically necessary covered care that the network cannot provide, **with plan authorization before the out-of-network care**.

For the network-insufficiency exception, the EOCs say the PCP or another network provider can contact the plan to obtain authorization for the out-of-network provider.

### Virginia call safeguard

Virginia's EOC explicitly says that if services are out-of-network without authorization the member can be responsible for the full cost and instructs the member to **contact Member Services before paying** if there is uncertainty about whether the service is covered.

### New Jersey family-planning exception

New Jersey's EOC specifically permits certain **Family Planning** services through out-of-network providers, with those services covered directly through Medicaid fee-for-service. Do not generalize that state-specific carve-out to the other plans.

## Provider leaves the network

When a provider leaves the network, the plan must help the member transition to a qualified provider. This can become a **continuity-of-care** issue rather than a simple provider-search call.

### Michigan `H9314-001`

At initial enrollment, the EOC says Aetna must maintain the member's current providers and services—including drugs/providers outside the network—while the new member's HRA/care plan is established; the duration depends on the service and reason. It says changes to services received at enrollment are considered only after the HRA is completed.

Operational trigger:

`NEW MI MEMBER + existing provider/service -> do not tell member to stop care merely because provider appears OON -> check transition/care-coordination rules.`

### Illinois `H9771-001`

The exact EOC is unusually specific:

- first time enrolling in a D-SNP: existing doctors may be used for **180 days**;
- changing from another D-SNP to this FIDE plan: existing doctors may be used for **90 days**;
- special circumstances can extend care longer; assigned Care Coordinator / Member Services handles the transition; and
- for a new enrollee's active course of treatment, the EOC says the plan may not require advance approval during the first **90 days**, including treatment that began with an out-of-network provider.

This is plan-specific; do not copy the 180/90-day Illinois transition periods to another state.

### Virginia `H1610-001`

The exact Medicare EOC reviewed does not provide the same Illinois-style initial-provider continuation periods. Use the current Virginia transition-of-care / Medicaid guidance and Care Coordinator for an active course of treatment rather than inventing a timeframe from another plan.

### New Jersey `H6399-001`

For a provider who **leaves the network**, the exact EOC gives strong continuity protections under certain circumstances:

- up to **4 months** beyond the provider's termination effective date;
- pregnancy: through postpartum evaluation, up to **6 weeks after delivery**;
- post-operative follow-up: up to **6 months**;
- oncology treatment: up to **1 year**; and
- psychiatric treatment: up to **1 year**.

If an in-network specialist is unavailable/inadequate for the member's medical needs, the NJ EOC says the plan must arrange an out-of-network specialist, with prior authorization obtained before care.

## Provider says "we don't take Aetna"

Do not resolve this from the provider's generic statement alone. The provider may participate with some Aetna products but not the member's **exact contract/network**.

Working branch:

`provider statement -> exact HIDE/FIDE plan -> current directory/internal network record -> provider location/NPI if available -> confirm participation for exact plan -> if mismatch, help locate alternate provider or route network discrepancy under current workflow`

Do not tell the member to pay out of pocket solely because the office says it is "out of network" until the exact plan/network status and any transition/authorization rights are checked.

## Provider bill + network status intersection

If the member gets a bill from a provider and the call reveals a network question:

`BILL -> claim -> service date -> provider network status on date of service -> authorization/continuity exception -> documented member responsibility -> provider correction / Chapter 7 / appeal`

Current network status today is not necessarily the same as network status on the **date of service**.

## Desk-aid version

**NEW PCP?** `exact network -> accepting new -> coordinated services? -> change through Member Services -> effective date`

**SPECIALIST?** `in-network specialist generally no PCP referral -> still check PA/order`

**OUT OF NETWORK?** `emergency/urgent/dialysis/network gap? -> authorization/exception -> do not promise coverage`

**PROVIDER LEFT NETWORK?** `continuity-of-care branch -> state/plan rule -> Care Coordinator`

**BILL + NETWORK QUESTION?** `network status on date of service -> claim/authorization -> member responsibility`
